Curatorial text

Best viewed in Netscape compiles four works created for the web browser in 1995-1997, when most people were accessing the web via dial-up modem. One regular aspect of HTML was chosen by each of the artists and turned into the medium for the piece. In Automatic Rain, JODI applied the blink tag to the blue text to make it flicker down a black screen. In LOVE, Michaël Samyn (working as Group Z, Belgium) layered small transparent GIFs on tiled background images so that even an image-heavy page would still load. Olia Lialina's My Boyfriend Came Back from the War uses frames that divide the window into smaller and smaller frames every time the reader clicks. And Alexei Shulgin used some of the most mundane components of a web page, buttons and checkboxes, to create Form Art.

Lialina and Shulgin both lived in Moscow, and Form Art is, in part, a reaction to JODI's HTML experiments, Rhizome says. In 1997 Lialina stated her rule: "If something is in the net, it should speak in net.language."

This language has evolved. The blink tag doesn't do anything in any current browser, and HTML5 lists frames as obsolete. On a fast connection JODI's rain also seems to drop in all at once; on a 1995 modem it trickled in. So the show presents each piece twice: a screenshot of the copy Rhizome keeps, and a small rebuild of the same feature, running in the browser you are using right now. The exhibition focuses on the difference between the two.

Automatic Rain

JODI (Joan Heemskerk and Dirk Paesmans), 1995

Three web pages: a red logo copied from the flags that marked timed lawn sprinklers at San Jose State University, a black screen of blinking blue text that fell slowly over dial-up, and a photo of rain on a window that the artists call Holland.

LOVE

Group Z, Belgium (Michaël Samyn), 1995

A grid of pages built from small layered GIFs that the viewer moves through with up, down, left and right arrows; most paths start at an interactive flower and end on the last frame of a love story. It was published on äda'web, and visitors could add their names to a running list.

Net Art Anthology page

My Boyfriend Came Back from the War

Olia Lialina, 1996

A hypertext story of a young woman meeting her partner after he comes home from a war. Clicking on phrases and images moves the story forward and divides the browser window into more and more frames.

Form Art

Alexei Shulgin, 1997

Animations and small games made only from HTML form elements, created during a residency at C3 in Hungary. Shulgin also ran a Form Art Competition with a $1,000 prize as a joke on contests like Prix Ars Electronica.
