Halwa & Sunlight

In our first apartment in Delhi, the walls were thin enough
to hear the whole week arriving —
neighbors gossiping, a stove ticking,
my grandmother's bangles keeping time in the next room,
counting down, without ever saying so, to Friday.

I. Every Friday Evening

Every Friday evening, right on schedule, the smell of halwa arrived before her voice did — ghee melting into sugar, into semolina, into something that smelled exactly like being loved.   I'd stand in the doorway, too short to see over the counter, just tall enough to steal a spoonful when she pretended not to notice. The whole apartment seemed to soften on those evenings, as if the week had finally exhaled.

II. What Friday Meant

It wasn't a holiday. Nothing about it was written down anywhere. But somehow everyone in that apartment knew: Friday was the day the pans came out bigger than usual, the day my grandmother hummed under her breath instead of just listening to the radio, the day the whole building seemed to smell faintly of celebration by six o'clock.

What She Kept in That Kitchen

  • A steel dabba that never seemed to run empty
  • A radio tuned permanently to old film songs
  • A single copper pan reserved only for Fridays
  • Enough patience to answer the same question four times

Her recipe for happiness (unwritten, but I remember it like this):

  1. Wait for Friday — don't rush it, even if the week was long
  2. Add more ghee than the recipe says you need
  3. Let the whole house fill with the smell before anyone asks what's cooking
  4. Serve it warm, in the smallest bowl you own, to whoever is standing closest

III. The Balcony After

After the halwa, we'd carry our bowls out to the tiny balcony, watching the streetlights flicker on one by one below us, the whole city settling into its own version of a Friday evening. My grandmother never said much out there. She didn't need to — some kinds of contentment don't require any words at all.

I don't remember the address of that apartment anymore, or which floor it was on — I remember it the way you remember warmth: not as a fact, but as a feeling that stayed, folded permanently into the shape of a Friday evening. If you're curious about the dish that meant home to me, you can read a little about halwa here, though no article gets the smell quite right.

Even now, whenever ghee hits a hot pan on a Friday evening, wherever I am, I am small again, standing in a Delhi kitchen, perfectly and simply happy.
