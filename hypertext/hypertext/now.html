Now: 7,000 Miles Away

My mornings at Rutgers start with an alarm on my phone, which is efficient and reliable and nothing like being called up a staircase. The room I wake up in now doesn't have four windows, or a patterned wall, or a desk that's ever quite as cluttered, no matter how hard I try. It's a decent room. It's just not that one.

Some mornings, half-awake, I still reach for the wrong wall with my eyes, expecting blue and brown instead of whatever's actually there. It passes in a second. But for that second, I'm back in that room, 7,000 miles from where I actually am, which is either a strange trick of memory or proof that some rooms don't really let you leave.
