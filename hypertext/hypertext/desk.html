The Desk

The desk was never clean, not once, not even the day I moved out of that room for good. There was always a textbook open to the wrong page, a pen that had stopped working days ago that I kept anyway, and a growing pile of things that belonged somewhere else in the house but had ended up there instead.

Most mornings I didn't sit at it at all before school. It was more of a nighttime place: half-finished homework, headphones in, the kind of quiet that only exists right before you're supposed to be asleep. But some mornings, running late, I'd stand in front of it for ten seconds deciding what to shove into my bag, and that decision felt more important than it should have.

From the desk I could glance toward the windows to check how bad I'd overslept, or turn the other way when I heard my mother calling, already annoyed before I'd even answered.

Now, 7,000 miles away, my desk is somewhere else entirely, and it's strangely, almost offensively, tidy.
