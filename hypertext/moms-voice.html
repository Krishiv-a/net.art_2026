My Mother's Voice

It always came from the bottom of the stairs, never from inside the room, as if she had a rule about it. The first call was gentle enough to ignore. The second one had my name in it. By the third, she was usually already partway up the stairs, and I knew I had about ten seconds left of pretending to be asleep.

I used to be annoyed by it, the predictability of it, the way it happened at almost the same volume every single day like an alarm that had learned to sound disappointed. It's strange what you miss. I don't think I appreciated, at the time, that someone was tracking my mornings that closely, that there was a person downstairs whose day started by making sure mine did too.

After the third call I'd finally get up, glancing back once at my desk before heading down.

Now, 7,000 miles away, nothing wakes me up on a schedule like that anymore, and some mornings that's the thing I notice missing before anything else.
