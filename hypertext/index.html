Waking Up, 7,000 Miles Away

The room was small enough that I could reach most of it without standing up. Three of the walls were plain white, the kind of white that disappears once you've lived with it long enough. The fourth wall was different: patterned in blue and brown, the one thing in the room that felt like it had been chosen on purpose.

At the far end, facing the door, were four big windows in a row, wide enough that the whole wall seemed made of light in the morning. Against one side of the room sat my desk, covered in the leftovers of whatever I'd been doing the night before. And every school morning, before I was ready for it, there was my mother's voice coming up the stairs, telling me it was time.

I don't think I ever once woke up on my own in that room. It was always a window, or a voice, or the light doing something I couldn't ignore. I didn't know, back then, that I was memorizing it.
