The Blue and Brown Wall

The pattern was busy, blue shapes overlapping brown ones, nothing symmetrical about it, and I used to lie in bed and trace the lines with my eyes until I'd made the same shape into a bird, a boat, a bad drawing of a face, whatever I needed it to be that day. My mother picked it out before I was old enough to have an opinion, and by the time I was old enough, I didn't want it changed.

It was the last thing I looked at before I got up and the first thing I looked at half-asleep, before my eyes adjusted to anything else. I never photographed it. I don't know why. Maybe because it felt like something that only worked if you were actually lying there, half-awake, with nowhere else to be yet.

From that side of the room I could turn my head and see the four windows, already doing their morning thing with the light.

Now, 7,000 miles away, I still picture it exactly, which is strange, because I can't picture most walls I've lived with since.
