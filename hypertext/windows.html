The Four Windows

Four windows in a row, opposite the door, so that walking into the room felt like walking toward daylight even on the days there wasn't much of it. In the summer the light came in early and hot, and I'd hear the street outside before I opened my eyes: someone's scooter, a vendor calling out the same two words he called out every morning, a dog that wasn't mine but somehow felt like it was.

In winter the same windows did the opposite. The light took its time, gray and reluctant, like it also didn't want to get up for school. I used to keep the middle two curtains half-open just so I could tell how bad the day was going to be before I even sat up.

Turning away from them, I could see the blue and brown wall on one side and my desk tucked against the other.

Now, 7,000 miles away, the light comes from somewhere else, and it took me a while to notice I was waiting for it to feel the same.
